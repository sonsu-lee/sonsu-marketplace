# Author style profile

Create `style-profile.md` only when the user asks to learn their style. Base it
on three to five prior pieces. With a small sample, describe tendencies rather
than certainties. Use the profile for later Japanese drafting and revision;
never let it override factual fidelity or source certainty.

## Source writing

List each sample's title, URL or supplied location, and writing date when known.

## Point of view

- First person: 私, 僕, 自分, none, or another observed form.
- Distance from the subject: participant or observer.
- Balance between assertions and reservations.

## Sentence endings

- だ・である, です・ます, or intentional mixture.
- Recurring endings, frequency of noun endings, and habits such as 「〜と思う」
  or 「〜のだ」.
- How strongly endings express emotion or stance.

## Rhythm

- Typical sentence lengths and their variation.
- Paragraph lengths and their variation.
- Punctuation habits, including frequent or sparse 読点.

## Vocabulary

- Recurring concrete words and metaphors.
- Favored or avoided loanwords and specialist vocabulary.
- Frequency of names and numbers.

## Expressions to avoid

Record only expressions the author explicitly rejected. Do not generalize a
personal preference into a universal rule. Capture the specific combination,
its context, and exceptions rather than banning an entire word.

For each observed preference, record:

- Expression or combination.
- Context in which to avoid it.
- Candidate alternatives.
- Exceptions worth retaining.
- Evidence: author comment or revision history.

## Structure

- Opening pattern: conclusion, incident, or another observed approach.
- Argument progression: conclusion first or cumulative; tolerance for digression.
- Ending pattern: summary or deliberately unresolved resonance.

## Overall tendencies

Summarize what distinguishes this writer and which observed tendencies matter
most when drafting with natural-japanese. Distinguish evidence from tentative
inferences and preserve the scope of each preference.
