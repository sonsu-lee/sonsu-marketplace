# Manual review when execution is unavailable

Use this checklist only when the environment cannot execute the bundled
scripts. A configured runtime's failure must be reported and resolved, not
silently bypassed. Disclose that mechanical checks did not run. Do not invent a
lint score. Follow the same preservation and disposition rules as
[the skill](../SKILL.md): every issue is fixed or retained with a reason.

Start with checks 1–3, then use 4–9 when the document warrants closer review.
The mandatory paragraph-level concrete-meaning review still applies when
editing; extract its units manually when tools are unavailable.

## 1. Formulaic expressions

Look for these expressions. Many can be deleted without changing meaning;
if deletion breaks the connection, rephrase using only source content.
Keep proper names, quotations, genuine factual meaning, and certainty.

- Forced conclusions: 「と言えるでしょう」「と言えるだろう」「と言えます」
  「ということになるでしょう」「のではないでしょうか」「結論から言うと」
  「結論として」「まとめると」「総じて」「いかがでしたか」「いかがでしょうか」.
- Emphasis: 「非常に重要」「極めて重要」「重要なのは」「大切なのは」
  「ポイントは」「言うまでもなく」「言うまでもありません」「まさしく」.
- Prefaces and bridges: 「さて」「それでは」「このように」「このような中」
  「ここで注目したいのは」「見ていきましょう」「紹介していきます」
  「解説していきます」「深掘りしていきます」「について見ていく」「を探求する」.
- Generic caveats: 「一概には言えません」「個人差がありますが」
  「あくまで一例ですが」. Preserve real uncertainty rather than deleting it.
- Attitude instead of substance: 「正面から扱う」 and its variants with
  「見る・書く・立てる・回収する」; 「不可欠」「核心的」「鍵となる」
  「根本的な」「多角的」「包括的」「総合的」「掘り下げる」「深掘りする」
  「言語化する」.

「重要なのは」「このように」「不可欠」「ポイントは」「さて」 also appear
in ordinary writing. One or two occurrences are not by themselves a problem.

## 2. Translation-like language

Replace English-shaped syntax with ordinary Japanese without changing certainty:

- 「〜という点で」「〜という観点から」 → 「〜では」「〜で見ると」.
- 「〜にとって重要」「〜にとって不可欠」 → 「〜には〜が要る」 when equivalent.
- Abstract 「〜を持つ」 → 「〜がある」.
- 「〜することによって」 → 「〜して」「〜すると」.
- 「〜であることは間違いない」「〜に他ならない」 → 「〜だ」 when equally
  definite. Place existing evidence first if useful; do not add evidence.
- Inanimate subjects with 「もたらす」「示す」「意味する」「証明する」: consider
  the actual person or situation, or 「〜から分かる」「〜を見ると」. Never
  invent a subject or change the claim's strength.
- 「それは〜である。なぜなら〜だ」: put the existing reason first or combine
  with 「〜だからだ」.
- 「〜することができる」 → 「〜できる」 or the verb's potential form.

## 3. Negation and contrast

Suspect overuse when 「〜ではなく〜」「〜だけでなく〜も」 occurs at least three
times, or at least four sentences end in 「〜ではない」「〜ではありません」
or 「〜わけではない」. Do not count questions such as 「〜ではないでしょうか」.

- Delete a gratuitous rebuttal of an unasserted assumption only when no
  factual, logical, or safety information is lost and the next sentence connects.
- For rhetorical 「AはBではない。Cだ。」, join the subject to the positive claim.
- Keep negation that narrows a meaning, corrects a real misconception,
  preserves a warning, or explains a necessary contrast.

## 4. Sentence lengths

Look for paragraphs whose sentences are nearly identical in length. In new
writing, divide or combine according to content. In existing writing, this
alone does not justify changing sentences. Do not insert short sentences only
for rhythm. Split multiple claims when clarity requires it.

## 5. Paragraphs

Check whether every paragraph has the same number of sentences. Match length
to substantive weight. If many begin with 「しかし」「また」「そして」, remove
connectors only where the content already expresses the connection.

## 6. Repeated openings and constructions

If the same opening or structure occurs three times consecutively, reorder
sentences or subjects, or combine related sentences. Do not add rhetorical
questions or noun endings merely to break the pattern.

## 7. Noun endings

No noun ending in a text over roughly 2,000 Japanese characters is an upstream
stylistic signal, not a requirement to add one. Genre and authorial intent take
precedence, particularly in technical or business prose.

## 8. Generic paragraphs

Look for paragraphs without names, figures, or examples. Gather better material
for a new draft. In revision, identify useful missing material rather than
inventing it. Do not manufacture firsthand experience.

## 9. Readability

Apply [the readability review](readability.md) manually. Prioritize double
negatives and nested conditions, multiple claims in long sentences, runs of
seven or more kanji, three or more 「の」, and embedded lists of at least three
parallel items. Preserve truth conditions, actors, technical terms, and
existing author voice.

## Structure

Manually extract headings and each paragraph's first sentence. Check the
argument, repeated forms, emphasis, the position of the conclusion, and
supported next actions where appropriate. Use the matching document-type
reference; sparse surface problems do not establish sound structure.

## Convergence and final comparison

Stop when each identified issue is fixed or retained with a reason and edits
introduce no unresolved new issues. If an issue recurs twice, reconsider the
sentence or paragraph or retain it with a reason instead of cycling synonyms.
Compare the final text with the original for added facts, numbers, evaluation,
policies, shifted attribution, changed certainty, and lost conditions. Label
this as manual review, not a successful script run.
