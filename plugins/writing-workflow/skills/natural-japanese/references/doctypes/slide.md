# Slide outlines: the messages alone must carry the argument

## Reader's question

Does reading the messages in order explain the path to the conclusion? Assume
the audience sees each slide for seconds and reads mainly its title and
prominent sentence. Put the claim in the message; evidence bullets support it.
This reference concerns structure and element roles. Use
[writing principles](../writing-principles.md) for sentence-level guidance and
the skill's preservation rules for all existing text.

## Required elements

Each slide has three parts:

- **Title:** a short topic label or noun phrase.
- **Message:** one sentence stating the slide's conclusion, immediately below
  the title in the most prominent position. Aim for about 50 Japanese
  characters. If multiple points make it long, retain the main one and move
  the rest to evidence or another slide. Preserve qualifications even when
  shortening; do not turn a limited result into a universal claim.
- **Evidence:** bullets supporting the message, preferably numbers and cases,
  not generic paraphrases. Let substantive weight determine the count; one
  bullet is enough when it supports the point.

## Structure

Use an introduction establishing the problem and goal, a body of no more than
three major evidence groups, and a conclusion identifying the requested next
action. With four or more evidence groups, develop the main two or three and
cover the rest lightly on one slide when that preserves the argument.

Read only the messages from first to last. Check whether:

- The argument can be followed.
- Each message actually states a conclusion.
- Sentence structures are varied rather than mechanically repeated.
- Important slides receive more substantive weight.
- The final slide connects to the requested judgment or action.

If the messages fail this check, polishing evidence bullets will not repair
the argument. Keep a text outline compact: title, message, and evidence lines,
not intervening prose paragraphs. Do not bold every 「タイトル:」「メッセージ:」
label or every evidence phrase; ubiquitous emphasis ceases to distinguish.

## Quality criteria

- Each message is a single sentence near 50 characters, not a long list of
  unresolved A/B/C items.
- It gives a conclusion rather than a label: 「新機能の概要」 is a topic, while
  「新機能で入力時間が半分になる」 is a claim requiring supporting evidence.
- Messages alone lead to the conclusion, even without evidence detail.
- Evidence consists of supplied numbers or examples, not 「効果が見込まれる」.
- Information density varies according to importance rather than every slide
  having the same number of lines and exactly three bullets.
- The ending says what the audience should decide or do, not merely
  「ご清聴ありがとうございました」. Do not invent an action during revision.

## Common failures

Putting 「導入のメリット」 or 「今後の展望」 in the message position leaves the
claim unstated until readers inspect every bullet. Filling missing material
with 「[サービス名]」「[想定コスト]」 produces a seemingly complete but empty
outline. Ask for essential missing information; do not present placeholders
as a completed deliverable. Exactly three bullets on every slide hides
substantive differences.

The most relevant principles are conclusion-first messages, the distinction
between a topic title and a conclusion message, uneven emphasis, and a final
supported next action.

## Sales-tool proposal example

Supplied material: entering the same deal separately into a CRM and quotation
system duplicates an average of 12 minutes per deal. A new tool copies CRM
entries into the quotation system. In a three-team pilot, average work time
fell by 1.8 hours per salesperson per week after four weeks.

A weak slide uses the title 「新営業支援ツールについて」 and the message
「新ツールの導入メリット」, then claims generic efficiency, smoother information
sharing, and customer satisfaction. The last two benefits are not in the
material, and the actual measured result is missing.

A faithful slide is:

```text
タイトル:案件情報の二重入力
メッセージ:二重入力をなくした試験導入で、営業1人あたり週1.8時間の作業が減った
- いまは CRM と見積システムに同じ情報を別々に入力しており、
  1案件あたり平均12分の重複作業がある
- 新しいツールは CRM への入力を見積システムに自動で反映し、この重複をなくす
- 3チームの試験導入で、4週間後に平均で週1.8時間の削減を確かめた
```

Keep 1.8 hours in both message and evidence rather than rounding upward for
impact. Say the pilot reduced time, not that every deployment will do so.
The three-team scope matters even when the headline is brief.

An introductory agenda can also preview a conclusion instead of listing only
「現状の課題」「新ツールの提案」「導入スケジュール」. When the proposal is
authorized by the material, it might say:

> 二重入力をなくした試験導入で、営業1人あたり週1.8時間の作業が減った。
> 全体への導入と、その時期を提案する。

Do not add the rollout proposal if the source contains only pilot results.

## Missing material

Requests often omit company names, costs, or dates. Ask which values to use
rather than inventing them or delivering 「約[金額]円」. If a fact cannot be
confirmed, explicitly mark it 【要確認】 and disclose that the affected part
remains unresolved; do not imply the outline is complete.

For a broad one-line request, establish the candidate conclusion and evidence
groups from context before filling slides. Ask about unresolved direction when
necessary instead of making a full deck around an unsupported assumption.

## Choose slide count from conclusions

Count meaningful evidence groups first, then add introduction and conclusion.
Do not allocate content into an arbitrary slide count and create filler or
crowd multiple claims onto one slide. Two genuine body groups do not require
inventing a third.
