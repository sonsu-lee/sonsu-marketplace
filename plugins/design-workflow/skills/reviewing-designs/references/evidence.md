# Conditional critique evidence

Use the relevant section when the requested critique needs research context.
These observations come from reviewed source passages and sampled frames, not
measured user outcomes or independent validation of the artifact under review. A
source illustrates a judgment; the current artifact must support the finding.

## Visual judgments

Compare emphasis with the user's actual task and required information. An
unattractive arrangement is not automatically a functional defect; explain which
action or content the hierarchy obscures. P01 is illustrated at
[211–303 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZT1UZQCTj_U&t=211s).

Assess an icon family together rather than enforcing a single construction rule
on isolated glyphs. Tiny solid details can remain coherent with an outlined
family. The P07 source's 372-second sample retains such a detail, while its
294-second sample shows the family comparison; the relevant narrated interval is
[363.58–387.029 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i8jG_5iKELI&t=363s).
This does not establish recognizability for the current users.

For a contrast claim, identify the actual foreground, surface and text
classification, then measure against the applicable standard. Do not infer
conformance from appearance or turn a source's example values into universal
rules. P20 source context:
[58–134 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8sMNvinRLu0&t=58s).

## Flow and state judgments

Separate what stays fixed, resizes and rearranges. Two endpoint screenshots do
not establish intermediate behavior, and hiding required content is not a
responsive fix. P16–P18 source context:
[91–138 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jaI2E50wu2k&t=91s),
[138–258 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jaI2E50wu2k&t=138s) and
[264–348 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jaI2E50wu2k&t=264s). Report
unobserved transitions rather than claiming to have exercised them.

Distinguish selection, current status and an available action. Check whether a
shown dependent choice remains compatible before recommending that it be
cleared. P28 context:
[207–249 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iHqhtvWn_0s&t=207s) and
[393–440 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iHqhtvWn_0s&t=393s). A
disabled control or generic error can support a concern about explanation or
recovery; it does not reveal an unseen backend policy or prove a broken request.

Associate progress and sources with the correct response and state. A rendered
success label is not proof that the system actually succeeded. P32 context:
[687–790 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ENIvZFD9iLM&t=687s). For empty
notification history, distinguish a real retention rule from absent data. The
P48
[486.39–512.27-second passage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Hzt7nv2ZBuo&t=486s)
and 496-second sample show explanatory retention/deletion copy, not enforcement
or measured comprehension.

## Conflicting references and handoff

If revisions leave references inconsistent, establish the approved change and
who owns it, then identify what should be synchronized. Still report defects
supported by the actual artifact; an outdated reference does not make every
difference a defect. W02 is an interview anecdote at
[978.24–1029.189 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9_ZOANs_UcU&t=978s),
not an audited incident or observed document update. In a read-only review,
recommend the correction without making it or inventing an approval hierarchy.

Trace a claimed improvement from the stated problem to the actual design choice
and available evidence. P46 context:
[966.36–1017.91 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Jzn4PnTmoU&t=966s). Do
not claim improved conversion, usability or collaboration from the narrative
alone, and do not turn this into a hiring or career-assessment rubric.
