## P44: Resolve the actual open design direction before further polish

Compare genuinely different directions along the unresolved decision axis before
polish. When repeated changes to layout, components or wording keep failing on
the same problem, treat that as a sign the cause is structural, such as what is
grouped together, which roles share one place or what counts as one product,
and stop producing surface variants. Present structural alternatives with their
trade-offs and what each keeps or moves, preserve existing functions unless the
owner decides otherwise, and let the owner choose. If every candidate gives up
a needed quality, test whether a different premise removes the trade-off before
choosing among them; do not reject every trade-off as a rule.

## P56: Preserve learned locations and habits through a redesign

For products people use repeatedly, keep the places, triggers and gestures they
have learned unless there is a specific reason to move them, and keep a
familiar trigger when the mechanism behind it changes. Propose large changes as
smaller releases ordered by priority and, where feasible, keep the previous path
available during the transition; when the old path closes is the owner's call.
Once a removal or move is approved, plan the transition for each existing user
state, such as never used, holding data or a balance, or relying on it often;
state who would lose what and propose a path for those still relying on it.
Whether to close something without such a path is the owner's call.
Overriding a platform or browser default to keep a habit is a separate
trade-off that needs its own check.
