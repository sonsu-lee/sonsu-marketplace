# Conditional graphic craft and handoff

Use this guidance for the matching construction or delivery decision. Source
observations are not evidence of current verification, measured outcomes,
production approval or a performed workflow. A toss.tech link marked "(session
transcript)" redirects to a conference session page whose transcript was the
source.

## Gotchas

- A source demonstration is not proof of usability or compatibility with every
  renderer.
- Do not assume a preview is the editable master or production sign-off.

## Icons and rendering

Judge the family together: silhouette, visual weight, corners and
recognizability at the intended size. Do not force every tiny detail to remain
outlined when it collapses. P07 context at
[363.58–387.029 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=i8jG_5iKELI&t=363s)
includes a 372-second sample with a tiny solid mark in an otherwise outlined
train icon; the 294-second sample supplies the family comparison.

Before a set grows or its production is handed to other people or a
generation tool, write its tone criteria in words, including what the style
leaves out and where it spends its finish. Draw a few representative samples,
place them on the actual product screens they will appear in, on light and
dark backgrounds where the product uses both, and only then extend the set in
the chosen direction. Decide facing, viewing angle and palette once for the
set instead of item by item. Where graphics sit inline with text, facing can
follow the reading direction, which reverses in right-to-left scripts. Source
context: [tone defined before delegation](https://toss.tech/article/ai-graphic-generator-1)
and [set criteria tried on product screens](https://toss.tech/article/22205).
Written criteria and sample placements are a brief and a check, not proof that
the set reads well to its audience.

Keep export bounds separate from optical construction. Preserve proportions, and
distinguish a path's bounds from the bounds contributed by its stroke. Do not
derive a universal pixel grid from a particular editor demonstration. P34
context:
[96.479–134.93 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vdp1zUqmYQc&t=96s) and
[391.56–417.83 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vdp1zUqmYQc&t=391s),
with the continuation at
[419.4–449.51 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vdp1zUqmYQc&t=419s).

Inspect the exported result in the actual renderer and scale, not just the
vector canvas. Simplify details that disappear while retaining meaningful
differences. P35–P36 context:
[254.76–299.03 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vdp1zUqmYQc&t=254s) and
[630.42–659.99 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Vdp1zUqmYQc&t=630s).
Neither the demonstration nor a local preview establishes usability for users or
compatibility with every renderer.

Check the largest intended size as well as the smallest. A drawing tuned for
small display can look sparse or unfinished when enlarged, for example where a
boundary is carried by a single line. When a set spans small UI and large
surfaces, inspect both ends. Before changing core proportions, consider
whether adjusting detail and volume is enough, since a large change can split
the graphic from the rest of its system. Source context:
[people graphics revised for large use](https://toss.tech/article/persona).
The source reports one team's visual judgment, not a measured legibility
result.

Judge identity candidates where they must work, not as an isolated symbol.
List the actual uses with the project; they commonly include small sizes such
as favicons and app icons, lockups with the wordmark, motion, headline media
and dense product screens. Render each candidate in those uses and report any
use not rendered as unchecked. A candidate that works in prominent mockups can
still look wrong in detailed interface. Source context:
[candidates compared in real uses](https://toss.tech/article/deus) and
[finals reviewed late on detailed UI](https://toss.tech/article/27392)
(session transcript). Which forms failed in those candidate sets is not a rule
against round, thin or any other form.

## Graphic meaning

When a graphic looks like data, make its intended mapping interpretable and
avoid implying magnitude or order that the content does not support. P45 source
context includes
[898.88–949.309 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Jzn4PnTmoU&t=898s) and
[259.06–320.19 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=65lKaReFEYA&t=259s).
These are a creator's interpretations of static examples, not proof that the
underlying data is wrong or that every viewer will misread it.

When illustrated people stand in for users, review whether skin tone, hair and
other features read as one particular race, culture or gender, against the
audience the product actually serves. A single "neutral" color may not exist,
or may clash with the surrounding interface; one alternative is a defined
range of tones mixed in scenes with several people. Do not fix a count of
tones or call any choice fully neutral; state what remains unresolved. Whether
people can see themselves in the figures is for the audience to tell, not a
review, and changing an established figure or brand character is the owner's
decision. Source context:
[people graphics and skin tones](https://toss.tech/article/persona).

## Preserve useful editable states

Before expansion, merging or another destructive authoring step, retain a
representation that is useful for anticipated editing, comparison or return.
This can be a meaningful stage, original group, live construction or version; it
is not a rule to duplicate every layer. Expansion and merging remain valid when
appropriate. Do not infer saved history or editability from appearance.

W05 context:
[244.32–328.76 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YWZGsn0T-sE&t=244s). The
309-second frame shows motif copies and selected path handles during the
live-shape explanation; captions provide the warning about premature expansion.
A still does not reveal every object's internal state. No recovery success, time
saving or improved quality was independently measured.

## Reproduction and delivery

When a real supplier or medium requires a monochrome treatment, consider the
complete identity, including symbol and wordmark, and retain the full-color
original. Do not prescribe a universal variant inventory or prohibit gradients
across all signage and print. Do not apply this advice to informative UI text or
icons merely because they use color.

W06 context:
[213.7–254.66 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5HEoiIVjX74&t=213s). The
228-second sample is an existing gradient identity presentation, not a completed
monochrome revision or manufacturing test. State which output, constraints and
production checks remain unverified.

When a print or manufacturing process shifts color or form, work back from its
stages: find where shifts occur, set starting values to compensate, and plan
physical samples at the stages that matter. When the size of a shift cannot be
predicted in advance, iterate stepped samples with the producer. A change of
shape can also meet the production equipment, so test variants on the actual
line. Without a sample from the real process, report the intended color or
form as unverified; a screen preview or supplier estimate does not establish
it. Source context:
[card color and shape samples](https://toss.tech/article/27176) (session
transcript).
This is a speaker's account of one production run, not compensation values or
evidence for another process.

When the agreed handoff includes an editable master and a corresponding
presentation, keep the master consistent with the treatment that presentation
shows for the delivery. If either artifact is unavailable, leave their
consistency unassessed. W01 context is
[581.44–601.829 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uJy-r9cgphg&t=581s).
This is the speaker's caution, not evidence that a correct editable deliverable,
client approval or calibrated output was inspected. Describe the representation
actually delivered; a preview is not the source file or a production sign-off.
